# Surface: /@lightjunction/web/

Mode: Experience. The visitor enters a real-time 3D ASCII nuclear scene through a slumped person on a chair. The user fixed the chair, person, first-person transition and choice actions; this personal artwork is independent of the bright `/@root/web/` brand.

The first view gives the chair and person the stage, with a sparse perspective floor beneath them. The first six seconds move the camera from third person into the seated person's eyes. At eight seconds a distant nuclear burst appears; at 8.5 seconds the visitor can move, stand or sit, turn away, face it, drink nearby coffee or seek the underground shelter behind the chair. The shockwave kills an unprotected player; actual room occupancy with the door fully closed provides a route to survival.

The world uses one runtime-generated ASCII projection with perspective, depth and surface lighting: 112 by 60 cells on desktop, 76 by 56 below 600px. The first-person view includes hands and a cup with walls, open rim and handle. A small Chinese heading, live status and native text buttons remain beneath the world. There is no introduction panel or promotional claim.

Walk down twelve steps from `z=-2` to `z=-5.05`, reaching `y=-2.6`; open the door, pass through it and close it from inside. The room spans `x=±2.6`, `z=-5.05..-9.6`, with a `y=-0.25` ceiling. Depth-filled analytical room surfaces and ground-opening occlusion keep it a real enclosed space. The protection condition requires room occupancy, a closed target and door angle `≤0.04` radians. Closing the door from outside or leaving it open inside must not save the player.

Keep the door closed until a survivor reaches aftermath at scene time 28 seconds. The survivor can then open it, walk back up the stairs and explore cratered ground and ruins with the same position and view. R voluntarily restarts. Death lowers the camera and automatically begins a new round after six seconds.

Controls: WASD or arrow keys move, Shift runs, desktop clicking requests relative mouse look and capture failure falls back to drag. Rightward mouse movement looks right, with pitch clamped to `±1.28` radians. E operates the nearby shelter door or changes stance elsewhere, B turns away, F faces the burst, C drinks near the table, P pauses and R resets. Esc releases capture. Compact layouts provide independent held A/W/S/D and run buttons, supporting multiple fingers alongside drag look; direct look cancels B/F turn targets. Disable movement and choices after death. Pause, visibility changes, blur, reset and death clear input and release capture; hidden pages stop time, while visible-page blur explicitly pauses until resumed.

Reduced motion keeps the opening chair and person still at time zero until “开始互动” is chosen. Reset and the next round repeat that explicit-start requirement while the preference remains active. A preference change to reduced motion pauses playback.

Constraints: one self-contained HTML file, one hash-pinned inline runtime combining `world_geometry.js` and `scene.js`, opaque-origin `sandbox allow-scripts allow-pointer-lock` without same-origin access; 7-bit world geometry, no WebGL, external assets, external requests or account actions. The author name is unclickable plain text; the surface has no links, navigation or forms. Canonical CSP explicitly blocks script attributes, connections, frames, objects and workers. Bind the final script hash to the deployed CSP. Do not replace the interactive world with recorded frames or a fixed-duration playback sequence.

`build.py` produces the shipping file and its hash/pin. `check_interactive_browser.py` defines desktop/mobile watch, open-room death, outside-closed-door death and inside-closed-door survival with aftermath walking and reset, plus reduced-motion lifecycle acceptance. Its `--pointer-lock-only` mode verifies a real native grant delayed past pause/resume is released, followed by fresh capture, relative look, Esc and reset. Independent QA adds true focus/visibility and multitouch checks. Recorded coverage is separate from a completed browser run, final design review and public signed publication; this brief makes no completion claim.
